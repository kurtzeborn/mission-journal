Questions

If the answer you need is not here, write to hello@pdayletters.com and a person will read it.

I'm a missionary and someone else set up my archive. How do I take control?

Email claim@pdayletters.com from your @missionary.org address. Anything in the subject and body — the message itself is the proof. We reply with a link; follow it and sign in with a personal account.

That makes you an owner of the archive, marked as the verified missionary. You can read, edit and delete any letter in it, and nobody can remove you or take your ownership away.

If somebody set the archive up who should not have it, open People and remove them. An owner can remove anyone except themselves, and the verified missionary is the one person no owner can remove — so once you have claimed it, the archive is yours to control.

Who can read an archive?

Only the people an owner has added. Archives are not public, are not listed anywhere, and are not indexed by search engines.

How do I add the rest of the family?

Open the archive, choose People, and enter their email addresses — one, or a whole list pasted in at once. We send each of them a link; nothing changes until they follow it. There is a limit of twenty invitations per archive per day, which is more than most families need in one sitting.

The address you type is only where the invitation is sent. Whichever account they actually sign in with is the one that gets access — which matters more often than you would think, because the address a family has used for somebody for twenty years is frequently not the account they sign in to things with.

What is the difference between a reader and an owner?

A reader can read the letters and download them. An owner can do that, and also edit or delete letters, invite other people, and remove them.

Owners can promote and remove each other. Nobody can remove or demote themselves, which is what stops an archive from ending up with no owner at all.

I forwarded a letter and nothing happened.

Almost always this means your mail program quoted the letter as text instead of attaching it. Try again using Forward as attachment — the exact menu item for each program is on the getting started page.

We write back once to say so. Only once, so that forwarding a stack of old letters does not produce a stack of replies — which does mean a second plain forward is met with silence.

Can the missionary sign in with their mission account?

No. @missionary.org accounts are managed by the Church and cannot be used to sign in here. Use a personal account — which is the right one anyway, since the mission account goes away at the end of the mission and the archive is meant to outlast it.

The mission account is still how a missionary proves who they are: it is the address they send to claim@pdayletters.com from. Proving and signing in are two different things here.

Does the missionary get emails from you?

Only if their archive does not exist yet. Once it has been set up, their letters are simply filed and they never hear from us — which is the point of a parent setting it up first.

What happens to letters nobody claims?

They are held, unpublished and unreadable, and then deleted: sixty days if the missionary sent them to us themselves, fourteen if somebody forwarded them. Every new letter that arrives pushes the date back.

Can I get the letters back out?

Yes. An owner can download the whole archive as a zip of the original emails, with the photos at full size. Nothing here is a one-way door.

How do I delete an archive?

Write to hello@pdayletters.com. There is no button for it yet, so it is done by hand — but it is done.

Why does the claim link expire?

Because it is a key. Anyone holding the link can set the archive up, so it is deliberately short-lived and stops working once it has been used. If yours has expired, send another letter to post@pdayletters.com and a fresh one follows.
