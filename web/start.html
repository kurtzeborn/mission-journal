Getting started

Two ways in, depending on who you are. Both end in the same place: letters arrive by email and are filed automatically from then on.

If you're a parent, family member or friend

This is the usual way to start, and it asks nothing of the missionary until the archive already exists.

  1. Forward one of their letters to post@pdayletters.com — as an attachment.

    This part matters, and it is the one thing people get wrong. Use Forward as attachment, not plain Forward:

    • Gmail — More (⋮) › Forward as attachment
    • Outlook — More actions (…) › Forward as attachment
    • Apple Mail — Message › Forward as Attachment

    A plain forward quotes the letter as text, and quoted text can be edited by anyone who passes it along — so there is nothing in it that proves the letter is really theirs. Attaching the original keeps the proof intact. If you forget, we will write back and remind you once.

  2. We reply with a link.

    Follow it and sign in. Use a personal account rather than a work or school one — the archive is meant to outlast the job you have today.

  3. The archive is yours.

    The letter you forwarded is published to it, and you can read it straight away.

  4. Ask the missionary to add post@pdayletters.com to their weekly letter.

    To, Cc or Bcc — any of the three works. That is the whole of what they have to do, and they never hear from us again: once the archive exists, their letters are simply filed.

  5. Forward the older letters to fill in the back catalogue.

    As attachments, the same way. Send twenty in one sitting if you like — they are filed by the date they were originally written, not the date you sent them, and you will not get twenty replies.

  6. Nothing after that.

    Every letter they send from then on appears on its own.

If you're the missionary

You do not have to set anything up — a parent can do all of the above without you. But if you would rather start it yourself, there are two ways.

  1. Simplest: Bcc post@pdayletters.com on your next letter home.

    We will reply to you with a link to set the archive up. Nothing is published until somebody does.

  2. Or, before you write anything: email claim@pdayletters.com from your @missionary.org address.

    Anything in the subject and body — we only need the message itself. Because it comes from your mission account, this proves who you are, and the link you get back carries that proof with it.

  3. Follow the link and sign in with a personal account.

    Not your mission account. It is managed by the Church and cannot be used to sign in here — and you lose it at the end of your mission anyway, which is exactly when the archive starts to matter.

  4. Then add post@pdayletters.com to the Bcc of your weekly letter.

    That is the last thing you have to remember.

What this does not do yet

Said plainly rather than discovered later: an archive is currently readable only by the person who set it up. There is no way yet to add the rest of the family, and it is the next thing being built.
