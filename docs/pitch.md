# Pday Letters

**Your missionary's weekly emails, saved in one place — automatically.**

---

## The problem

Every week, a letter arrives. Then it slides down the inbox.

Two years later there are a hundred of them scattered across email accounts, and half of them are gone — deleted, lost in a phone upgrade, or buried where nobody will ever scroll. Grandma never got most of them. Nobody can find the one about the baptism in Manaus.

These are some of the best things your missionary will ever write. They deserve better than an inbox.

## The idea

Once the archive exists, add one address to the weekly email. That's the whole recurring task.

Your missionary BCCs `post@pdayletters.com` on their letter home — or, if they'd rather not think about it at all, a parent just forwards it after it arrives. Either way, within a minute or two it becomes a post on a private letters site: the full letter, every photo, in order, all in one place.

No app or login for the missionary, and nothing new to remember on a P-day when computer time is already too short. A parent can create the archive before departure or after letters have already begun arriving.

## How it works

**A private site, just for your family and friends.** Owners decide who's on the list — grandparents, aunts, the friend from home. They sign in with the Google or Microsoft account they already have. Archives are never public; service operators can access them only for the support, safety, and legal purposes described in the privacy policy.

**Search that actually works.** Type a city name or "baptism" or "Sister Alves" and find the letter in a second. Two years of letters, instantly searchable — something no email inbox has ever quite managed.

**Photos included.** Saved with the archive in web-friendly form, in a browsable album alongside the letters they came with.

**Old letters welcome.** Already six months in? Anyone on the list can forward the letters they've been saving and they'll drop into the timeline in the right order. Duplicates sort themselves out, so three relatives forwarding the same letter still makes one post.

**A printed book whenever you're ready.** An owner can turn the archive into a hardcover book with a chosen cover and optional Foreword and Afterword. After the owner creates a private checkout, family members can order directly from the printer, which receives the book, takes payment, and handles shipping.

**And a copy that's yours.** Download the entire archive as a single folder that opens in any browser, works with no internet, and searches just the same. Put it on a drive, hand it to your kids. Whatever happens to this service, your letters are still yours.

## Now in beta

Pday Letters is live and serving real archives. It remains in beta while more families use it through the ordinary rhythm of a mission and the first production book completes the printing and delivery process.

Using it means adding one address to the weekly email, forwarding any older letters you've saved, and telling us what's confusing, broken, or missing. The online archive is free; printed books are optional purchases from the printer. Your archive stays private, and you can download a portable copy at any time.
